Fan-beam & helical geometry

Pick a parallel-beam ray — a direction through a point — and the 2D fan-beam view finds the source that measured it. The synchronised 3D scene shows why that ray is usually missing in a helical cone-beam scan: the source sits off the slice plane.

Helical cone-beam view

Fan-beam to parallel-beam rebinning. You pick a parallel-beam ray — a direction plus a point it passes through — and the demo finds the fan-beam view that actually recorded it. In a 2D scan that ray always exists. On the right the matching source is placed on a helical cone-beam path: it sits off the slice plane, so the ray it really measures tilts out of plane and the in-plane ray you asked for is usually missing.

Left — 2D fan-beam slice

  • Slider, or mouse-wheel over the canvas: set the projection angle.
  • Drag inside the blue circle: move the red point (a position in the field of view).
  • Green — the requested ray (fixed direction through the red point) and the detector it reaches.
  • Magenta — the source; it orbits the circle, positioned so its fan ray is the requested ray.
  • Red — the edges of the fan beam that just covers the field of view.

Right — 3D helical cone-beam

  • Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom. Starts looking down the rotation axis, matching the 2D view.
  • The blue grid is the slice being reconstructed — fixed at z = 0.
  • Source and detector ride the magenta helix, climbing one pitch per turn.
  • The yellow arrow is the ray from source to the red point — tilt the view and for most angles it leaves the slice plane.
